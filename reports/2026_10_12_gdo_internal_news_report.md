## 7. Scientific literature findings

Lighter-effort pass; a handful of recent items across forecasting, climate, surveillance and vaccine access.

- **Forecasting:** Researchers at Kerala's Institute of Advanced Virology have built a machine-learning Dengue Early Warning System (DEWS) combining surveillance data with rainfall, temperature and humidity to generate weekly district-level forecasts, sorting risk into four categories (very high/high/moderate/low); trained on ~6 years of data (March 2020–February 2026). Reported as still a research system requiring further validation before operational use. *Reported 2 Oct 2026.* [Current Affairs Adda247, Kerala develops AI-based dengue early warning system](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/kerala-develops-ai-based-dengue-early-warning-system-to-predict-district-level-trends/amp/)
- **Forecasting / deployment:** An Australian-led dengue outbreak early-warning tool is being trialled in Vietnam's Mekong Delta from early 2026 — one of the first such systems to be deployed at real-world scale rather than tested retrospectively, directly relevant to Viet Nam's own current-season rise flagged in recent snapshots. [Scimex, news briefing: dengue outbreak warning tool tackles growing threat to Australia](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/news-briefingdengue-outbreak-warning-tool-tackles-growing-threat-to-australia)
- **Climate drivers:** A Lanzhou University-led modelling study (presented at EGU 2026, 12 climate models) projects a substantial poleward expansion of dengue-suitable zones from tropical into temperate regions, with risk rising faster in China and the US than in Europe under most scenarios — though under the high-emission scenario some currently high-burden tropical areas (Brazil, India) may see reduced suitability due to extreme heat stress, a less commonly reported nuance. [EGU General Assembly 2026, abstract EGU26-4784](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-4784.html)
- **Vaccine access:** Takeda signed an exclusive deal (18 Sep 2026) with Dr. Reddy's Laboratories to distribute Qdenga (TAK-003) in India's private market, following CDSCO's 21 July approval (India's first approved dengue vaccine, ages 4–60). Availability expected in the first half of 2027; inclusion in India's public immunisation programme remains a longer-term ambition with price not yet announced.
